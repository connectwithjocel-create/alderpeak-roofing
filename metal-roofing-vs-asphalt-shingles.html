Metal Roofing vs. Asphalt Shingles

It’s one of the most common questions we hear when a roof needs replacing. Both are good systems. The better choice depends on your budget, your home and how long you plan to own it.

Upfront cost

Architectural asphalt shingles cost less to install than standing-seam metal. Metal’s higher cost comes from materials, custom-formed trim and specialized labor. If budget is the deciding factor, shingles usually win.

Lifespan and long-term value

A well-installed standing-seam roof generally lasts considerably longer than an asphalt shingle roof. If you plan to stay in the home for many years, the longer service life can offset the higher initial cost. If you may sell in a few years, that advantage matters less.

Heat and Texas sun

Metal with reflective finishes can reduce the heat the roof absorbs. Asphalt shingles age faster under intense heat and UV. In both cases, attic ventilation has a big effect on comfort and roof life.

Hail

Neither is hail-proof. Impact-rated shingles resist damage better than standard ones. Metal resists punctures but large hail can leave cosmetic dents. Panel thickness and finish influence how visible those dents are.

Check your HOA firstSome neighborhoods restrict metal roofing or specific colors. Get approval in writing before you commit to a material.

Appearance

Shingles offer a traditional, textured look and a wide range of colors. Standing-seam metal reads as clean and contemporary, and complements farmhouse and modern architecture. Many homes combine the two — shingles on the main roof with metal over porches.

Noise

Over solid decking with underlayment and an insulated attic, metal roofs are generally not noticeably louder than shingles in rain.

Maintenance

Metal needs very little routine maintenance beyond checking sealants at penetrations and trim. Shingles need periodic checks for lifted tabs, cracked pipe boots and worn flashing.

The bottom line

Choose shingles for lower upfront cost and broad flexibility. Choose metal for long service life, low maintenance and a modern look. A professional assessment will confirm what your roof structure, slope and HOA allow.
