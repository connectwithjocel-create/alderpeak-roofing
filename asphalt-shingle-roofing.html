03 Weighing it up

Where it excels. Where it doesn’t.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Lower initial cost than metal or tile
  • Widely available colors and profiles
  • Straightforward repairs and replacements
  • Impact-rated options offer greater hail resistance
  • Suits most residential architecture

Limitations & considerations

  • Shorter lifespan than metal or tile
  • Heat and UV accelerate aging in Central Texas
  • Vulnerable to hail bruising and wind lift
  • Poor attic ventilation shortens shingle life
  • Older colors may be discontinued, complicating repairs

04 Installation considerations

Details that decide performance.

Whatever the material, the details below separate a roof that performs from one that leaks early.

  1. 01

    Nailing pattern

    Wrong placement is a leading cause of wind blow-offs.

  2. 02

    Ventilation balance

    Intake and exhaust must match to protect shingles.

  3. 03

    Valley method

    Closed-cut, woven and open metal valleys behave differently.

  4. 04

    Impact rating

    Class 4 rated shingles resist hail better than standard.

  5. 05

    Color and heat

    Lighter shingles may absorb less heat.

  6. 06

    Starter strip

    Proper starter courses seal the eave and rake edges.

05 Frequently asked

Asphalt Shingle Roofing questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How long do asphalt shingles last in Texas?

It varies widely with product, installation quality, ventilation and storm exposure. Heat and hail tend to shorten lifespans compared with milder climates.

What are impact-resistant shingles?

Shingles tested to resist damage from hail impacts. They cost more but can perform better in hail-prone areas; some insurers offer considerations for them — check with yours.

Architectural or 3-tab shingles?

Architectural (laminated) shingles are thicker, more dimensional and generally more wind-resistant; they’ve largely replaced 3-tab for residential work.

Can I install new shingles over old ones?

Codes may allow a second layer in some cases, but tear-off lets us inspect and repair the deck, and avoids hiding problems.
