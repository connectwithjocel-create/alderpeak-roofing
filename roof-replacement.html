01 Overview

When replacement is the right call

Replacement makes sense when problems are widespread rather than isolated: brittle or curling shingles across multiple slopes, repeated leaks in different areas, significant hail damage, or a roof that has simply reached the end of its service life. It also makes sense when you want to change materials — for example moving from shingles to standing-seam metal.

We’ll walk you through material options, explain what’s included in the scope, and identify unknowns up front — like how deck repairs found during tear-off are handled — so there are no surprises mid-project.

A typical replacement includes

  • Removal of existing roofing down to the deck
  • Deck inspection and replacement of damaged sections
  • New underlayment and ice-and-water protection at critical areas
  • New flashing, drip edge, pipe boots and ventilation components
  • Installation of the chosen roofing system
  • Cleanup, magnetic nail sweep and final walk-through

02 Common problems

What we usually find.

These are the conditions we see most often on this kind of work in Greater Austin.

  1. 01

    End of service life

    Widespread granule loss, cracking and curling across slopes.

  2. 02

    Repeated leaks

    Multiple leak locations that point to system-wide failure.

  3. 03

    Severe storm damage

    Hail or wind damage too extensive for practical repair.

  4. 04

    Multiple layers

    Older roofs installed over previous roofs, adding weight and hiding problems.

  5. 05

    Deck deterioration

    Soft, delaminated or rotted decking from long-term moisture.

  6. 06

    Material change

    Upgrading appearance, performance or maintenance profile.

04 Weighing it up

Benefits and limitations.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Resolves widespread wear and recurring leaks at once
  • Chance to upgrade ventilation, flashing and underlayment
  • Can improve curb appeal and suit a new architectural direction
  • Resets the maintenance clock on your roof

Limitations & considerations

  • Significant investment that should follow a thorough assessment
  • Deck repairs discovered during tear-off can change cost
  • Noise and site activity during installation days
  • Material lead times and weather affect start dates

05 Frequently asked

Roof Replacement questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How do I know it’s time to replace rather than repair?

If problems appear across several areas, the shingles are brittle or bald, or the roof has had repeated leaks, replacement is usually more economical than continued repairs. An inspection will make the distinction clear.

Can a new roof be installed over the old one?

Building codes limit layers and an overlay hides deck problems. We generally recommend removing the old roof so the deck can be inspected and repaired.

What happens if you find rotten decking?

We’ll show you the damage with photos and explain how deck replacement is priced before proceeding — this should be agreed in your scope in advance.

How do I choose a material?

Consider budget, roof pitch, architectural style, HOA rules, and how long you plan to own the property. Our materials guide compares the options.
