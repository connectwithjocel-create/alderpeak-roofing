What to Check After Severe Weather

After a hailstorm or high winds, it’s natural to want to know if your roof is okay. Here’s what you can check safely — without a ladder — and what to document.

Safety firstDo not climb onto your roof after a storm. Surfaces may be wet, slippery or weakened, and falls from roofs and ladders cause serious injuries. Stay clear of downed power lines and report them to your utility.

1. Walk the property from the ground

Look for shingles, pieces of flashing or ridge caps in the yard; obviously missing shingles or exposed underlayment; and tree limbs on the roof.

2. Check the soft metals

Gutters, downspouts, roof vents, window screens, AC units and mailboxes show hail impacts clearly. Dents in these items suggest the roof was hit too.

3. Look at downspout outlets

Heavy deposits of granules after a storm can indicate shingle damage.

4. Check inside

Look at ceilings, especially near exterior walls and around light fixtures, for new stains. If your attic is safely accessible, check for damp insulation or wet decking with a flashlight.

5. Document everything

Take dated photos of damage, hail size (next to a coin or ruler), and any interior water. Note the date and time of the storm.

6. Protect against more water

Move belongings away from leaks and catch drips. If a ceiling bulges with water, keep people out of the area. A roofing professional can install temporary protection — please don’t attempt it yourself on the roof.

7. Get a professional assessment

Hail damage often isn’t visible from the ground. A roof assessment documents conditions up close so you can decide on repairs and whether to contact your insurer. Coverage decisions are made by your insurance company under your policy.
