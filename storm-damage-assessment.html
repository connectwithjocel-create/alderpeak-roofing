01 Overview

Careful documentation after severe weather

Central Texas regularly sees hailstorms and severe thunderstorms with damaging straight-line winds. Hail can bruise shingles, knock granules loose and dent metal; wind can lift and crease shingles, break seals and peel back edges. Some of this damage leads to leaks right away. Some shortens the roof’s life without leaking for months.

Our storm assessments document damage to the roof covering, soft metals (vents, gutters, flashing), and other exterior components with dated, labeled photos. We explain what we see and what it means for the roof — so you can make informed decisions, including whether to contact your insurance company.

A note on insuranceCoverage decisions belong to your insurer and depend on your policy. We document conditions; we don’t promise outcomes or interpret your policy. Read what to check after severe weather.

02 Common problems

What we usually find.

These are the conditions we see most often on this kind of work in Greater Austin.

  1. 01

    Hail bruising

    Soft spots and fractured mats in shingles, often under intact-looking granules.

  2. 02

    Granule loss

    Dark circular marks and granules collecting at downspout outlets.

  3. 03

    Dented soft metals

    Damage to vents, gutters, flashing and metal panels.

  4. 04

    Creased or missing shingles

    Wind lifts tabs, breaking the seal and creasing the shingle.

  5. 05

    Displaced ridge caps

    Ridge and hip caps are especially exposed to wind.

  6. 06

    Impact from debris

    Branches and blown objects that puncture or crack the roof covering.

04 Weighing it up

Benefits and limitations.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Dated documentation of conditions after a specific event
  • Identifies hidden damage before it becomes a leak
  • Helps you decide on next steps with clear information
  • Temporary protection guidance when openings are found

Limitations & considerations

  • Not all hail impacts damage a roof; some marks are cosmetic
  • Insurance decisions rest with the insurer
  • High demand after major storms can affect scheduling
  • Interior water damage may need other specialists

05 Frequently asked

Storm Damage Assessment questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How soon after a storm should I get an assessment?

As soon as practical — ideally within days or weeks. Prompt documentation makes it easier to tie damage to a specific event, and catching openings early limits water damage.

Can hail damage a roof without causing leaks?

Yes. Hail can bruise or fracture shingles and loosen granules without an immediate leak, reducing the roof’s remaining life.

Should I climb up and look myself?

Please don’t. Post-storm roofs can be slippery and structurally compromised. Look from the ground, take photos, and check the attic and ceilings.

Will you deal with my insurance company?

We can provide documentation and meet with an adjuster at your request, but we don’t make coverage promises. Your policy and your insurer determine coverage.
