01 Overview

Roofing for the homes of Greater Austin

Austin-area homes range from 1950s ranch houses to new hill-country builds with complex rooflines and mixed materials. Each brings its own details: low-pitch additions on older homes, standing-seam accent roofs over porches, tile on Mediterranean-style houses, and long valleys on contemporary designs.

Our residential work covers repairs, partial and full replacements, and upgrades to the parts of the roof that are easy to overlook — flashing, attic ventilation, drip edge and gutters. Every project starts with a documented assessment so you know what you’re paying to fix.

What residential work includes

  • Roof inspections for owners, buyers and sellers
  • Leak tracing and repair
  • Replacement planning with shingle, metal or tile
  • Ventilation, flashing, and gutter improvements

02 Common problems

What we usually find.

These are the conditions we see most often on this kind of work in Greater Austin.

  1. 01

    Aging shingles

    Granule loss, curling and brittle tabs that no longer seal down in summer heat.

  2. 02

    Flashing failures

    Leaks at chimneys, sidewalls and skylights where sealant has dried and metal has shifted.

  3. 03

    Poor attic ventilation

    Trapped heat that bakes shingles from below and drives up cooling demand.

  4. 04

    Mismatched past repairs

    Patches with incompatible materials or fasteners that create new leak points.

  5. 05

    Pipe boot cracks

    Rubber collars around plumbing vents that split in UV exposure and let water run down the pipe.

  6. 06

    Overflowing gutters

    Clogged or undersized gutters that push water back under the drip edge and into fascia.

03 How we assess it

How we approach it.

Exterior survey

Roof covering, ridges, valleys, edges and every penetration, photographed as we go.

Attic check

Decking, ventilation paths and signs of moisture or past leaks, where safely accessible.

Drainage review

Gutters, downspouts and how water leaves the roof and the foundation area.

Written findings

Annotated photos and options sorted by urgency, from repair to replacement.

04 Weighing it up

Benefits and limitations.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Protects structure, insulation and interiors from water intrusion
  • Opportunity to correct ventilation and flashing details while work is underway
  • Material choices that suit the architecture and HOA guidelines
  • Clear documentation useful for future sale or insurance conversations

Limitations & considerations

  • Hidden deck damage may only be found during tear-off and can change scope
  • Material lead times and weather can affect scheduling
  • Some HOAs restrict colors or materials and require approval first
  • Matching discontinued shingles for repairs isn’t always possible

05 Frequently asked

Residential Roofing questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How long does a residential roof replacement take?

Many single-family replacements take one to a few working days once materials are on site. Roof size, complexity, weather and any deck repairs found during tear-off all affect timing; we’ll give you a realistic schedule during planning.

Do I need to be home during the work?

Not necessarily, but we’ll ask for access to power outside, a clear driveway for deliveries and debris, and a quick walk-through at the start and finish.

Will you check my attic?

Where it’s safely accessible, yes. The attic often shows the earliest signs of leaks and reveals whether ventilation is working.

Can I change materials when I replace my roof?

Often, yes — for example from shingles to standing-seam metal. Tile is heavier and may need a structural review. HOA rules may also apply.
