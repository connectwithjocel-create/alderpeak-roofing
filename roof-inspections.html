Roof inspections you can actually read.

Whether you’re buying, selling, managing a property, or just haven’t looked up in a while, an inspection tells you where your roof stands — with photos and plain-language findings.

01 Overview

What an Alderpeak inspection covers

We assess the roof covering, every penetration and transition, the edges and drainage, and — where it can be reached safely — the attic. Findings are photographed and sorted by priority so you can see what needs attention now, what can be planned, and what’s simply normal wear.

Inspections are useful at specific moments: before buying a property, before listing one, after a severe storm, when a roof passes the 12–15 year mark, or when you notice a stain or a sound you don’t recognize.

You receive

  • A written summary of roof condition
  • Annotated photos of each finding
  • Priority ratings: urgent, plan for, monitor
  • Options explained without pressure

02 Common problems

What we usually find.

These are the conditions we see most often on this kind of work in Greater Austin.

  1. 01

    Hidden flashing wear

    Sealant failures and loose metal that aren’t visible from the ground.

  2. 02

    Early hail bruising

    Damaged shingle mats that look fine at first and fail later.

  3. 03

    Ventilation imbalance

    Too little intake or too much exhaust, shortening shingle life.

  4. 04

    Exposed fasteners

    Backed-out nails and screws that create small leak paths.

  5. 05

    Deck sagging

    Soft spots that suggest long-term moisture or structural concerns.

  6. 06

    Drainage problems

    Clogged valleys, gutters and downspouts directing water toward the house.

03 How we assess it

How we approach it.

Ground walk

Overall roof lines, gutters, fascia and signs of movement.

Roof surface

Covering, ridges, valleys, penetrations and edges, where safe to walk.

Attic

Decking, ventilation and moisture indicators.

Report

Annotated photos and prioritized findings.

04 Weighing it up

Benefits and limitations.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Catches small issues before they become interior damage
  • Useful documentation for buying, selling or insurance discussions
  • Helps you budget for repairs or replacement ahead of time
  • Gives a baseline to compare against after future storms

Limitations & considerations

  • Steep, wet or fragile roofs (like tile) may limit walkable areas
  • Some conditions inside walls or under membranes can’t be seen directly
  • An inspection is a snapshot of conditions on that day
  • Insurance coverage decisions are made by your insurer, not the inspector

05 Frequently asked

Roof Inspections questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How long does an inspection take?

Most residential inspections take about an hour on site, depending on roof size, pitch and attic access.

Is a roof inspection different from a home inspection?

Yes. A general home inspection covers the whole house and may view the roof from the ground or a ladder. A roofing inspection focuses specifically on the roof system in more detail.

Can you inspect a tile roof?

Yes, with care. Tile can crack under foot traffic, so we limit walking and use other viewing methods where appropriate.

Should I get an inspection before selling?

It can help — you’ll know about issues before a buyer’s inspection raises them and can decide whether to repair or disclose.
