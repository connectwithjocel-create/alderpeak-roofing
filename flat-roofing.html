Flat roofing depends on drainage.

Low-slope roofs don’t shed water by gravity alone. Membrane choice, seam quality, slope-to-drain and detailing at edges and equipment decide how well they perform.

02 Anatomy of the system

How a flat roofing system is built.

  1. 01

    Structural deck

    Steel, concrete or wood deck, depending on the building.

  2. 02

    Insulation + tapered slope

    Rigid insulation, often tapered to direct water to drains.

  3. 03

    Membrane

    Single-ply TPO or PVC with welded seams, or multi-ply modified bitumen.

  4. 04

    Edges and flashing

    Parapet flashing, edge metal, curbs and drain assemblies.

03 Weighing it up

Where it excels. Where it doesn’t.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Efficient coverage for large or complex footprints
  • White membranes can reduce rooftop heat gain
  • Accessible for equipment service and inspections
  • Recover options may be possible when conditions allow
  • Repairs can be localized

Limitations & considerations

  • Ponding water shortens membrane life
  • Punctures from foot traffic and trades are common
  • Seams and penetrations need regular inspection
  • Wet insulation can hide under an intact-looking surface
  • Requires more planned maintenance than steep-slope roofs

04 Installation considerations

Details that decide performance.

Whatever the material, the details below separate a roof that performs from one that leaks early.

  1. 01

    Slope to drain

    Tapered insulation or crickets to avoid ponding.

  2. 02

    Seam welding

    Heat-welded seams on TPO/PVC must be probed and tested.

  3. 03

    Equipment curbs

    Proper curb height and flashing around HVAC units.

  4. 04

    Parapets

    Terminations and coping that keep water out of walls.

  5. 05

    Walkway pads

    Protection paths for regular service traffic.

  6. 06

    Overflow drainage

    Secondary drains or scuppers in case primary drains clog.

05 Frequently asked

Flat Roofing questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
Is a flat roof really flat?

No — properly designed low-slope roofs have a slight pitch, often created with tapered insulation, so water flows to drains, gutters or scuppers.

TPO or PVC?

Both are heat-welded single-ply membranes. PVC tends to resist oils and chemicals better (useful near restaurant exhaust); TPO is widely used and often less expensive. Conditions decide.

Can a flat roof be repaired instead of replaced?

Often. Localized seam, flashing and puncture repairs are common. If insulation below is wet over large areas, replacement or partial removal may be needed.

Do homes have flat roofs?

Some do — modern designs, porches and additions. They need the same drainage attention as commercial roofs.
