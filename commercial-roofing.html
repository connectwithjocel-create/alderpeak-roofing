01 Overview

System-level thinking for commercial roofs

Most commercial buildings in Greater Austin use low-slope roofs covered by single-ply membranes such as TPO or PVC, modified bitumen, or metal roof systems. Their performance depends less on the field of the roof and more on seams, drainage, and the details around rooftop equipment, parapets and penetrations.

We provide condition assessments, targeted repairs, recover and replacement planning, and maintenance programs that help property managers budget instead of react. Communication with tenants and building staff is planned into the work.

Commercial scope

  • Roof condition assessments with photo documentation
  • Membrane seam, flashing and curb repairs
  • Recover versus tear-off evaluation
  • Drainage corrections for ponding water
  • Flat roofing systems and metal roofing

02 Common problems

What we usually find.

These are the conditions we see most often on this kind of work in Greater Austin.

  1. 01

    Ponding water

    Standing water more than a day or two after rain, which ages membranes and finds weak seams.

  2. 02

    Open seams

    Membrane laps that have separated from heat cycling, foot traffic or poor original welding.

  3. 03

    Curb and penetration leaks

    HVAC curbs, pipes and conduits are frequent sources of interior leaks.

  4. 04

    Parapet and coping failures

    Loose coping, cracked wall flashing and termination bars that let water behind the membrane.

  5. 05

    Trade damage

    Punctures and cuts from equipment service and rooftop foot traffic.

  6. 06

    Wet insulation

    Hidden moisture under the membrane that reduces insulation value and adds weight.

03 How we assess it

How we approach it.

Document conditions

Membrane, seams, flashing, drains and rooftop equipment, mapped and photographed.

Find the moisture

Identify likely wet areas and leak paths before recommending repair or recover.

Prioritize

Separate urgent repairs from planned capital work so budgets match risk.

Plan around operations

Schedule work with tenant access, safety and building hours in mind.

04 Weighing it up

Benefits and limitations.

An honest picture helps you choose with confidence.

Benefits

  • Extends roof life through targeted repair and maintenance
  • Reflective membranes can reduce rooftop heat gain
  • Documented conditions support capital planning
  • Recover options may avoid a full tear-off when conditions allow

Limitations & considerations

  • Recovering is only appropriate when the existing roof is dry and sound
  • Rooftop equipment may need to be lifted or disconnected by other trades
  • Large projects require staging, permits and tenant coordination
  • Some leaks originate in walls or equipment, not the roof

05 Frequently asked

Commercial Roofing questions.

Straight answers to the questions property owners ask most. If yours isn’t here, send it to us.

Ask a question
How often should a commercial roof be inspected?

A common practice is twice a year — typically spring and fall — plus after major storms. Regular inspections catch open seams and clogged drains before they become interior leaks.

What is a roof recover?

A recover installs a new roof system over the existing one instead of tearing it off. It can save time and cost, but only when the existing roof and insulation are dry and code allows another layer.

Do you work with property managers?

Yes. We provide documentation, prioritized recommendations and scheduling that suits building operations and tenants.

Which membrane is best: TPO, PVC or modified bitumen?

Each has strengths. The right choice depends on rooftop traffic, chemical exposure (for example restaurant exhaust), budget and existing conditions. See our flat roofing page for a comparison.
